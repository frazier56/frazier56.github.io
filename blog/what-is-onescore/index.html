What Is OneScore? Published Scores, Connections and Proof Levels

Learn where OneScore displays published scores, how to review connection proof levels, and what its simulator estimates.

If you're a working professional — a photographer, DJ, event planner, barber, contractor, or any kind of freelancer — your reputation is probably spread across five or more platforms. You have reviews on Google, ratings on Yelp, endorsements on LinkedIn, followers on Instagram, and testimonials buried in Facebook comments.

None of them talk to each other. A client considering hiring you has to piece together your credibility from scratch every single time. Most won't bother — they'll hire whoever shows up first with enough stars.

This is the problem OneScore was built to solve.

What exactly is a OneScore?

OneScore displays a published score alongside information about connected accounts. Review the available evidence and proof labels to understand what a profile shows.

The OneScore leaderboard orders scored public profiles by published score. OneJob also uses published score to order its professional listings, alongside search and location filters.

OneScore is part of One World Labs. It displays your published score and the proof levels associated with connected accounts.

Information to review

When considering a professional, review the information available on their profile. These are review suggestions, not a list of confirmed scoring inputs:

  • Connected accounts — review the proof level shown for each connection.
  • Reviews & ratings — read the feedback and consider its source and context.
  • Work history — look for relevant examples and ask about completed projects.
  • Communication — discuss availability and expectations before agreeing to work together.
  • Profile details — check whether the information addresses what you need to know.
  • References — ask for relevant references and review the evidence available.

In the simulator, an asset’s class and proof level affect its estimated contribution, and repeated assets in the same class have diminishing returns.

Using OneScore when reviewing a professional

Review the evidence behind the profile

Review the person’s profile and the proof labels on their connected accounts. A score alone does not establish that every claim has been verified.

Look at the connected accounts

Check the proof label on each connection rather than assuming that every connected account has the same level of evidence.

Keep the context in view

Consider the available evidence alongside the work you need done. A published score does not replace your own review of a professional.

Use the score alongside other information

Review the profile and available evidence when deciding whom to work with.

Reviewing your profile and simulator estimates

  1. Connect relevant accounts and review their proof levels. The simulator estimates potential changes; an additional connection may add no points.
  2. Keep your profile clear — provide relevant information about your work, location and skills.
  3. Ask for feedback after a job — useful feedback can help others understand your work. Our guide on getting more five-star reviews explains how to ask. This does not guarantee a score increase.
  4. Set communication expectations — agree on how and when you will respond to clients.
  5. Review the available evidence — check connection proof levels and address any missing information.

Put the score in context

A published score is one piece of information. Review the profile, connection proof levels and relevant work before making a decision.
