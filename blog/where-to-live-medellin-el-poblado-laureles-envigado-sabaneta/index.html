Where to live in Medellín: El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado or Sabaneta?

By Lee Frazier, founder of One World Labs, Medellín. Published 25 November 2026; last updated 25 November 2026.

Short answer

El Poblado is the most expensive of the four: one 2026 report puts its rent at COP 53,812 per square metre per month in July 2026, about COP 4.84 million for a 90 m² apartment. Laureles, Envigado and Sabaneta cost less, and a site we checked lists typical unfurnished one-bedroom rents of roughly COP 2.2 to 3 million in Laureles, 1.7 to 2.5 million in Envigado and 1.5 to 2.2 million in Sabaneta. Treat every figure as a guide and compare live listings before you decide.

How much has rent in El Poblado risen?

The Medellín cadastral observatory (OIME), quoted by El Colombiano on 9 November 2026, puts rent in El Poblado at COP 53,812 per square metre per month in July 2026, against COP 15,851 in 2014. For a 90 m² apartment the paper calculates a move from about COP 1.43 million to about COP 4.84 million a month, and a real (inflation-adjusted) rise of 57.5% between 2022 and July 2026 [1]. The paper notes that its base of 8,066 rental observations is not an official rent index [1].

What is each area like?

These are general descriptions, not measurements, so visit before you sign. El Poblado is the best-known area for visitors and newcomers, with the most restaurants, nightlife and furnished apartments, and the highest prices. Laureles (including the Estadio side) is a flatter, residential area that many people choose for everyday life and lower prices. Envigado and Sabaneta are separate municipalities south of the city; they are generally quieter and cheaper, so check the commute to where you will work or study.

Which area is cheapest to rent in?

On the one source that lists all four, Sabaneta has the lowest typical one-bedroom range and El Poblado the highest [3]. A second site gives wider all-barrio ranges for April 2026: COP 3 to 8 million for El Poblado, 2 to 4 million for Laureles and 1.5 to 3.5 million for Envigado, and labels them as indicative [4]. The two sites do not use the same measure, so do not average them.

Is it better to rent or buy?

A 7.9% gross yield in El Poblado and 7.4% in Laureles looks high, but it is an asking-price calculation that leaves out building fees, property tax, vacancy and management [2]. If you plan to stay a few months, renting is simpler. If you are thinking of buying, speak to a Colombian lawyer first; this guide does not cover the legal steps.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the most expensive area of Medellín to rent?

Of these four, El Poblado. Rent there reached COP 53,812 per square metre per month in July 2026, according to OIME data reported by El Colombiano [1].

How much is rent in El Poblado?

The OIME figure implies about COP 4.84 million a month for a 90 m² apartment [1]. One site lists unfurnished one-bedrooms at roughly COP 3.5 to 5 million [3]; another gives COP 3 to 8 million across the area [4].

How much is rent in Laureles?

One site lists COP 2.2 to 3 million for an unfurnished one-bedroom and 2.7 to 3.5 million for a two-bedroom [3]; another gives COP 2 to 4 million across the area [4].

How much is rent in Envigado?

About COP 1.7 to 2.5 million for an unfurnished one-bedroom and 2.3 to 3.5 million for a two-bedroom on one site [3]; COP 1.5 to 3.5 million on another [4].

How much is rent in Sabaneta?

One site lists COP 1.5 to 2.2 million for an unfurnished one-bedroom and 1.9 to 2.8 million for a two-bedroom [3]. We found no second source, so check live listings.

Is El Poblado worth the extra cost?

It depends on what you value. You pay more for being close to restaurants, nightlife and furnished options; if you work remotely and want a quieter, cheaper base, another area may suit you better.

Are these prices official?

No. The OIME figure is from the city's cadastral observatory but the paper says it is not an official rent index [1]; the other rent figures are approximate figures from websites.

Do furnished apartments cost more?

Usually yes. The ranges above are for unfurnished apartments. See our guide to furnished, monthly and short-term apartments.

How often do these numbers change?

Often. Rents in El Poblado rose in real terms by 57.5% between 2022 and July 2026 [1]. We will update this guide when newer data is published.

Does One World give legal advice on renting?

No. This guide is general information, not legal advice. Read your contract and get advice from a Colombian lawyer when it matters.
