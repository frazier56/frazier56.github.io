Buying a home in Medellín: houses for sale, owner financing and building

What to check, what to ask the seller, and why a local lawyer belongs in the room.

Short answer

Look at several homes, confirm who legally owns the one you want, agree the price and the payment terms in writing, and have a Colombian lawyer review the contract before you pay a deposit. Some sellers will accept payment in instalments (owner or seller financing); the terms vary, so everything about it needs to be written down and checked by a lawyer. This is general information, not legal advice.

Houses and apartments for sale

Used homes can be visited and compared; new apartments sold off plan are bought from a developer before they are finished, so check the developer's record and the delivery date in writing. On OneHome you can browse homes for sale in Medellín and message the sellers and agents directly.

What to check before you pay

  • Who owns the property, and whether it has any debts or claims on it. Ask a lawyer to review the ownership documents.
  • The condition of the building and any building-wide charges (administración).
  • That the person you are dealing with is the owner or an authorised agent.
  • The exact price, the currency, what is included, and the date of the deed signing.

Owner (seller) financing: what to ask

  • How much do you pay up front, and how much in instalments?
  • What is the interest rate, if any, and what is the payment schedule?
  • What guarantees the seller has, and what happens if you miss a payment?
  • When do you receive the deed, and how is the debt recorded?

Do not sign or transfer money until a lawyer has read the agreement and explained these points to you.

Building on your own lot

If you plan to build, you will need plans, a professional such as an architect or engineer, a building permit and a crew. For labour prices and what to put in the contract, see hire a builder or contractor in Medellín.
