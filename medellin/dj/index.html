What changes the price

  • Sound and lights: a DJ who brings speakers, a mixer and lighting charges more than one who plays on the venue's system.
  • Hours: most quotes are per set or per hour; ask what an extra hour costs before the night.
  • Date: December, long weekends and Saturdays book up first.
  • Travel: venues outside the metro area (Llanogrande, Guatapé, Santa Fe de Antioquia) usually add transport.

How hiring through OneJob works

  1. Find the person. Search pros in OneJob, or open a profile someone sent you. Profiles show what the person does, their photos and videos, and reviews from past jobs.
  2. Put it in writing. Start a contract with the date, hours, place, what is included and the price, in pesos or dollars. Both of you accept it in the app.
  3. Pay your way. Card payments made through OneJob on eligible contracts are held and released after you both mark the job complete. To pay a person in pesos with Nequi or PSE, use OnePay, part of the same app, by link or QR code. Cash or a direct transfer is up to you, but OneJob does not hold that money.
  4. Leave a review. After the job, each of you reviews the other, so the next person can decide with more to go on.

Questions people ask

How much does a DJ cost in Medellín?

On booking sites, DJs in Medellín typically list 45 to 120 US dollars an hour, so a four-hour party runs about 180 to 480 US dollars. A five-hour wedding DJ with sound is commonly quoted at 1.5 to 5 million pesos. Each DJ sets their own price.

Does the DJ bring the sound system?

Some do and some play on the venue's system. Put it in the contract: who brings speakers, the mixer, microphones and lights, and how long setup takes.

How far ahead should I book?

For December and long weekends, book as early as you can. For a normal weekend, a few weeks is usually enough, but popular DJs fill up first.
